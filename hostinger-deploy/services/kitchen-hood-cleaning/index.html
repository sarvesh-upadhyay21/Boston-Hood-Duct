Commercial Kitchen Hood Cleaning & Fire Prevention

Why Professional Kitchen Exhaust Cleaning Matters

Commercial cooking produces grease-laden vapors that accumulate inside hood canopies, plenums, grease ducts, filters, and rooftop exhaust fans. Without proper inspection and cleaning, these combustible deposits can increase fire risk, contribute to regulatory violations, and cause costly operational disruptions.

A visibly clean hood canopy does not necessarily mean concealed ductwork or rooftop fans are free of grease.

BOSTON HOOD & DUCT goes beyond surface cleaning, focusing on accessible exhaust system components, grease removal, service documentation, and identification of conditions requiring corrective action.

Our objective is to help restaurant owners and facility managers reduce grease-related fire hazards, maintain inspection-ready exhaust systems, and support ongoing fire code compliance.

NFPA 96 & Massachusetts 527 CMR 1.00 Fire Code Requirements

Commercial kitchen exhaust systems in Massachusetts are regulated under 527 CMR 1.00, Chapter 50, including applicable NFPA 96 provisions. Massachusetts requires appropriate individual Certificates of Competency and business Certificates of Registration for regulated commercial cooking exhaust inspection and cleaning services. Commercial kitchens in Boston must also meet applicable Boston Fire Department requirements.

Inspection vs. Cleaning Intervals

The schedule above sets mandatory inspection intervals. Actual bare-metal grease cleaning is triggered when grease depth exceeds NFPA 96 thresholds (typically 50 microns / 0.002 in). In Greater Boston, local municipal fire prevention regulations may mandate accelerated cleaning frequencies for high-capacity kitchens.
